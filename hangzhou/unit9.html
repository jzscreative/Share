Tsientang University

A bottled-water fortune is building a new research university in the tea hills southwest of the city, designed to disappear into a forest.

In this unit you will learn
  • who Zhong Shanshan is and how a water company paid for a university
  • how a foundation became an institute and then a university in the making
  • the steps a new university has to pass in China
  • where the campus is, how big it is, and what the architects designed
  • who is building it, and when it is meant to open
Words to know
Foundation
A non-profit body set up to hold money and spend it on a stated purpose, such as a school.
Institute for advanced study
A research body with scientists and few or no undergraduates, modeled on the one in Princeton.
Master plan
The overall layout of a large site: where buildings, roads and open space go, before each building is designed.
Phase
One stage of a building project. Phase one is built first; later phases can wait years.
Photovoltaic (PV)
Panels that turn sunlight directly into electricity.
Atrium
A tall open space in the middle of a building, usually lit from above.
Local design institute (LDI)
A licensed Chinese design firm that turns a foreign architect's design into official construction drawings and takes them through government review.

9.1 The founder

Tsientang University (钱塘大学, Qiantang Daxue) is being paid for by one man: Zhong Shanshan (钟睒睒), the founder and chairman of Nongfu Spring, China's biggest bottled-water company, and of its parent company Yangshengtang. Nongfu Spring's red-capped bottles are in every shop in Hangzhou, and its headquarters is in the same southwest corner of the city as the campus, at 181 Geyazhuang in Xihu District; the institute that is founding the university gives the same address.

His story. Zhong was born in December 1954 in Zhejiang; sources differ on whether in Hangzhou or in Zhuji, the family's home town to the south. He left school after fifth grade, during the Cultural Revolution, and worked as a bricklayer and then a carpenter. When China's college entrance exam came back in 1977 he sat it twice and missed the cutoff both times, then studied Chinese literature at a television university, a correspondence college that taught by broadcast. In 1983 he became a reporter on the farm desk of the Zhejiang Daily, the provincial newspaper, and stayed about five years. In 1988 he left for Hainan, the island province that had just been opened as a special economic zone like the Shenzhen zone in Unit 4, where growing mushrooms and farming shrimp lost money. His first success was selling another company's health tonic as a regional agent. In 1993, at 38, he founded Yangshengtang in Hainan, a health-products company whose first hit was a pill made from turtle. In 1996 he came home to Zhejiang and founded the company that became Nongfu Spring, first named for Qiandao Lake west of Hangzhou, where its first plant drew deep, clean water. Its slogan, "Nongfu Spring is a little sweet," arrived in 1998; its sales passed 10 billion yuan ($1.5 billion, €1.3 billion, £1.1 billion) in 2012. Yangshengtang also bought a struggling Beijing vaccine maker, Wantai, in 2001, and Wantai went on to make China's first home-grown vaccine against cervical cancer, licensed in 2019.

The money. Nongfu Spring listed on the Hong Kong stock exchange on 8 September 2020, and Zhong has been near the top of China's rich lists ever since. The Hurun list of October 2025 put him first in China at 530 billion yuan ($80 billion, €71 billion, £59 billion), a record for that list, with about 80 percent of it coming from Nongfu Spring. The company sold 42.9 billion yuan ($6.4 billion, €5.8 billion, £4.8 billion) of drinks in 2024 from twelve water sources around the country, Qiandao Lake among them. Zhong seldom talks to the press; Chinese media call him the "lone wolf," and he has described himself as "a loner" who does not care what his competitors are doing.

On 22 January 2025, at Yangshengtang's annual meeting, Zhong announced that he would give 40 billion yuan ($6.0 billion, €5.4 billion, £4.5 billion) over ten years to found a new university. Chinese press reports say he had the idea as early as 2016. He said the money would come from "Yangshengtang's accumulated resources and the contributions of every employee here." His stated aim is a "new" university "that China does not yet have": one that does "0 to 1" discovery in basic science and then turns it into technology people can use, "one leg in science, one in technology."

Key numbers
  • 40 billion yuan ($6.0 billion, €5.4 billion, £4.5 billion) pledged over ten years
  • 10 billion yuan ($1.5 billion, €1.3 billion, £1.1 billion) paid into the foundation so far
  • 530 billion yuan ($80 billion, €71 billion, £59 billion) Zhong's fortune on the Hurun list, October 2025
Did you know?

40 billion yuan ($6.0 billion, €5.4 billion, £4.5 billion) is a lot even for a university. Westlake University, Hangzhou's other new private research university, was started with gifts from 36 founding donors, many of them entrepreneurs, plus land and buildings from three district governments; Fuyao University in Fuzhou was promised about 10 billion yuan ($1.5 billion, €1.3 billion, £1.1 billion) by the glass maker Cao Dewang. Unit 10 compares them.

9.2 From a foundation to a university

A university in China cannot simply be declared. It grows through bodies that each need official approval. Tsientang has three so far:

  1. The Hangzhou Tsientang Education Foundation (钱塘教育基金会), approved by the city's Civil Affairs Bureau in May 2021. Its stated purpose is to fund a high-level private university. The money goes here first; the institute's website says the first 10 billion yuan ($1.5 billion, €1.3 billion, £1.1 billion) has been credited to the foundation's account, with the full 40 billion arriving over 10 to 20 years.
  2. The Zhejiang Qiantang Institute of Basic Science Research (浙江钱塘基础科学研究院), set up with 100 million yuan ($15 million, €13 million, £11 million) from the foundation. Sources disagree on its start: November 2023 in one report, registered on 11 January 2024 in the city's approval records. Zhong is its legal representative.
  3. The Tsientang Institute for Advanced Study (浙江钱塘高等研究院, TIAS), formally registered in July 2025. This is the body the architects name as their client and the one that signed the construction contract. It calls itself "the founding institution for a new research-intensive university." Whether it is the earlier institute under a new name is not stated anywhere we could find.

What kind of university. The agreement between the city and the foundation (June 2025) describes it in four phrases: high-level planning, research-driven, internationally oriented, and "small but excellent." Its fields are science, engineering and work that crosses between them. TIAS lists four research areas: physical sciences, life sciences including biomedicine, AI and informatics, and humanities and social sciences. One commentary in University World News (July 2026) reports a plan of about 3,000 students in total, with more doctoral students than undergraduates, no traditional departments, and about 60 laboratories; that is a single source, so treat the numbers as the plan as reported, not a fixed design.

The first students. On 6 September 2026, TIAS welcomed its first 14 doctoral students, in a joint program with Zhejiang University: a year of coursework at Zhejiang University, then thesis research at TIAS, with a Zhejiang University degree at the end. Their fields are physical sciences, life sciences, medicine, AI and integrated circuits. The ceremony was held at TIAS's Longwu campus, its current home in the Longjing tea region, not on the new site. No date has been published for the first undergraduates.

The people. No president has been named. The best-known names are two physicists from Peking University. Chen Dongmin (陈东敏) is deputy director of the institute; he went to the United States in 1981 as one of the first Chinese physics students selected through the CUSPEA exam, worked at Brookhaven National Laboratory and Harvard, co-founded a photonics company in Silicon Valley, and returned to run laboratories in Beijing and Dongguan. Wang Enge (王恩哥) chairs the institute's Academic Committee; he was president of Peking University from 2013 to 2015 and a vice president of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. The institute reports 82 staff, 21 named faculty, and more than a dozen "incubation projects" (its English and Chinese pages count 13 and 14), one of which is new kinds of specialized lighting.

Did you know?

The motto on the institute's website is three words: "Truth, Freedom, Gratitude."

Look for it in Hangzhou

TIAS's Longwu campus is in the Longjing tea region, in the hills southwest of West Lake, the same tea country as Longjing village in Unit 8.

9.3 The government's steps

Private money starts a university in China, but the city, the province and finally the Ministry of Education have to let it happen. Official notices still call the school 钱塘大学（暂名）, "Qiantang University (provisional name)." Here are the steps so far, in order.

The step that has not happened, as far as anyone has published, is approval by the Ministry of Education in Beijing, which is what turns a project into a university that can enroll its own students and grant its own degrees. When the Ministry listed ten proposed new schools in June 2025, Qiantang was not on it. That is why the first doctoral students are enrolled through Zhejiang University and why the name still carries "provisional."

Did you know?

Westlake University, whose first campus is in the same corner of Xihu District, went through the same ladder: a research institute first (2016), then Ministry approval as a university (2018), then its first undergraduates (2022). Unit 10 has the story.

9.4 The site

The campus is in the Zhuantang area of Xihu District, the hilly, forested southwest corner of Hangzhou, west of the West Lake scenic area and on the north bank of the Qiantang River. On the Unit 8.1 map, that is the far southwest, upstream from Qianjiang New City and the tidal-bore stretch of the river. Within a kilometer to the south are two arts schools, the China Academy of Art's Xiangshan campus and the Zhejiang Conservatory of Music; to the north and east is a golf course, and the forested hills of Xishan National Forest Park, beside Longwu tea village, are next door. TIAS describes the site as "beside Longwu Tea Garden," in the Longjing tea region.

How big. Sources do not agree, and the honest answer is that they count different things. The architects and their engineers say 475,000 square meters. The province approved 36.3 hectares, which is 363,000 square meters. The city's February 2026 planning notice counts 346,592 square meters of land across three phases. The institute's own site says "about 35 hectares." The larger figure probably includes land outside the built parcels, but no source says so. The floor areas in the planning notice are firmer:

Worked example

How big is a 346,592 square meter site?

  1. 346,592 square meters is 0.35 square kilometers, or about 35 hectares (about 86 acres).
  2. A soccer field is about 7,140 square meters. 346,592 ÷ 7,140 ≈ 49 fields.
  3. Central Park in New York is 3.41 square kilometers. 0.35 ÷ 3.41 ≈ 0.10, so the site is about a tenth of Central Park.
  4. Using the architects' 475,000 figure instead gives 67 fields and a seventh of Central Park.

The campus is about the size of 49 to 67 soccer fields, depending on whose boundary you draw.

Did you know?

"Tsientang" is an old spelling of Qiantang, from the postal system used before pinyin. The institute, the architects and the engineers all use it. Some famous Chinese universities keep old spellings the same way: Tsinghua University in Beijing is "Qinghua" in pinyin, and Peking University is "Beijing" University. Chinese newspapers write 钱塘, Qiantang.

Key numbers
  • 346,592 m² of land in the city's planning notice
  • 399,492 m² of floor space across three phases
  • 20 m height limit for the buildings
  • 3,000 new trees
Look for it in Hangzhou

A January 2026 visit found the site fenced in dark green barriers with excavators clearing the ground. Phase one has 893 days of construction in its contract, about two years and five months.

9.5 The campus plan

The master plan is by Renzo Piano Building Workshop (RPBW), the Italian firm behind the Centre Pompidou in Paris and the Shard in London, with the design institute of Zhejiang University (UAD) as the local design institute producing the official drawings. RPBW's partner in charge is Luigi "Gigi" Priano. Design began in 2023. In April 2026 the Chinese architecture site Archiposition published the scheme under the title "Piano's plan under construction: Qiantang University, hidden in the forest."

Two plots. The academic plot holds ten buildings of laboratories and offices, with exhibition space on their ground floors, around a central "academic commons" meant to be the social heart of the campus. Three public buildings, an auditorium, a sports center and a library, line the street edge around a public piazza that the neighborhood can use. The residential plot holds 23 buildings set into the landscape, close to the university but kept quiet and private.

A campus in a forest. The architects' big idea is that the forest is the subject and the buildings are the background: the campus is conceived as a forest setting, with the buildings kept low among the trees.

The buildings. Every building follows the same rules, which is what makes the campus read as one place: nothing rises above 20 meters, so the buildings stay below the tree canopy; ground floors are lifted and set back to keep the footprint small, so the buildings seem to float among the trees; the design is meant to be transparent, with light, views and movement passing between spaces; and every building carries a photovoltaic roof, with a central energy center serving the campus.

RPBW already has one finished project in Hangzhou: OōEli, also called Tianmu Li, finished in 2020 after seven years of design and construction. Sixteen office buildings, most of them nine stories, surround a central park of 90 by 120 meters with a piazza, gardens and a shallow pool that can be drained for concerts and films. The same ideas run through the university plan: buildings of one height around a shared open space, with trees and gardens between them.

Did you know?

Knippers Helbig of Stuttgart, specialists in steel and glass structures, handle the structure and facades.

9.6 Who is building it, and when

The builder. On 30 December 2025 the Tsientang Institute for Advanced Study signed the phase-one construction contract with Zhejiang Provincial Construction Engineering Group, a state-owned builder, for a provisional 1.8 billion yuan ($270 million, €242 million, £202 million). The city's notice gives phase one 893 days of construction, about two years and five months. In January 2026 the site was being cleared, and a project official estimated about three years to completion.

The design team is international. RPBW in Genoa leads the architecture, with about 20 people on the project. UAD, the design institute of Zhejiang University in Hangzhou, is the local design institute for the architecture. Knippers Helbig in Stuttgart, specialists in steel and glass structures, handle the structure and facades. The architects' July 2025 booklet also lists Arup (London) for mechanical and electrical engineering, ADCAS (Beijing) for the laboratories and Atelier Ten (New York) for sustainability.

When it opens. No opening date has been published. The contract's 893 days from the start of 2026 points to mid-2028.

Look for it in Hangzhou

Zhejiang University's main Zijingang campus is in the northwest of the city; UAD, its design institute, is the local architect of record for the new campus.

Check your understanding

  1. Who is paying for Tsientang University, and what business made the money?
    Show answer

    Zhong Shanshan, founder of Nongfu Spring, the bottled-water company, and its parent Yangshengtang. He pledged 40 billion yuan ($6.0 billion, €5.4 billion, £4.5 billion) over ten years.

  2. Why are the first doctoral students getting a Zhejiang University degree?
    Show answer

    The university does not yet have Ministry of Education approval to enroll students and grant degrees, so the institute runs a joint program with Zhejiang University.

  3. Name the three bodies that have been set up on the way to the university.
    Show answer

    The Tsientang Education Foundation (2021), the Qiantang Institute of Basic Science Research (2023 or 2024) and the Tsientang Institute for Advanced Study (2025).

  4. What is the height limit for buildings on the campus, and why?
    Show answer

    20 meters, so the buildings stay below the tree canopy and the campus reads as a forest.

  5. Why do sources give different sizes for the site?
    Show answer

    They count different things: the architects' 475,000 square meters probably includes land outside the built parcels; the province approved 363,000; the city's planning notice counts 346,592 across the three phases.

About this guide

About this guide. Hangzhou in Context is a private study guide for the Buro Happold lighting team on the Tsientang University project, so that people arriving on the job know the city, the country and the campus. It is not a Buro Happold publication and not a project deliverable; it is not for the client, the design team or the public, and nothing in it should be quoted as the firm's view.

Facts were checked against the sources listed at the end of each unit as of the dates shown. Statistics change every year, rankings have newer editions, and some campus details come from published design material that will be revised, so treat every figure as a snapshot. Money is converted at one fixed rate, given on the contents page. Photos are credited to their sources and are used here for study only; do not copy them into other documents.
